## 1. The Pipeline

Build this in 2 days, then run it daily.

**Daily cadence (90 min):**
- 20 min — 5 hyper-tailored apps (quality > quantity)
- 20 min — LinkedIn "1-hour trick" (see §3)
- 20 min — 2 cold outreach DMs/emails to recently-promoted people
- 15 min — Follow-ups + thank-you emails from yesterday
- 15 min — 1 networking action (event, post reply, coffee chat)

**Weekly (Sunday, 2 hr):** refill the funnel with 40–60 new roles.

**Metrics that matter:**
- Apps → first response: target >15%
- First response → interview: target >40%
- Interview → offer: target >20%

If a rate is below target, that's where your funnel leaks. Fix only the leak.

## 2. Sourcing Channels (ranked by ROI)

### Tier S — daily
1. **Startups under 50 people** — Wellfound, YC's Work at a Startup, Pallet boards, a16z / Sequoia portfolio job pages, talent collectives. April is peak startup hiring.
2. **Google for Jobs** — `"software engineer" "new grad" 2026 site:greenhouse.io` (also `lever.co`, `ashbyhq.com`). Filter past week.
3. **LinkedIn jobs with ≤10 applicants, posted past 24h.** Hit within 2 hours of posting.
4. **Simplify** — autofill + sourcing, but apply via the company ATS directly for priority targets.

### Tier A — weekly
5. Hacker News "Who is hiring?" (first of every month)
6. 30 handpicked startup career pages in a sheet
7. University Handshake
8. Domain-specific Discords / Slacks

### Skip or deprioritize
Indeed, ZipRecruiter, LinkedIn Easy Apply on posts older than 3 days.

## 3. The LinkedIn 1-Hour Trick

Three times a week:

1. Search `"software engineer" OR "SWE"` + city/remote, filter **past 24 hours** + **under 10 applicants**.
2. For each match, find the hiring manager or an engineer on the team (not the recruiter).
3. Apply through the company ATS (not Easy Apply), then:
   - Send a personalized connect note under 200 chars referencing the role and one specific thing
   - Follow up 3 days later with a short value-add message

Fresh posts + low applicants + warm human touch > 2000 cold apps.

## 4. Target Recently-Promoted People

**Why:** new budget, new team to staff, motivation to prove the promotion.

**How to find:**
- LinkedIn filter by company → sort by "recently promoted"
- Post search `"excited to share" "promoted"` + company name
- LinkedIn alerts for target companies

**Template:**
> Hi [name], congrats on the [role] promotion — saw your post about [specific project]. I'm a [background] engineer focused on [their stack/domain]. Not asking for a referral cold — I'd love to hear what problems your team is prioritizing this quarter, and whether any of what I've built ([one-line portfolio]) looks useful. Happy to send a resume only if it seems like a fit.

Response rate: 15–25% vs. <2% for "can I get a referral."

## 5. Mirror the Posting Language Exactly

ATS and human screeners both keyword-match.

- JD says "React," don't write "React.js." JD says "CI/CD," don't write "DevOps."
- Copy the JD's section headers into your bullets: "build scalable systems" → "Built scalable…"
- Match seniority verbs: startups like "owned / shipped / drove"; big cos like "collaborated / designed / implemented."
- Mirror their skills-section ordering.

This is exactly what `resume-tailor` should be doing on autopilot.

## 6. The Thank-You Email Nobody Sends Correctly

Send within 4 hours. One per interviewer. Not a group email.

```
Subject: Thanks — [role] interview

Hi [name],

Thanks for the conversation today. Two things stuck with me:

1. Your point about [specific technical thing they said] — I went back
   and looked at [related thing], and I think [substantive 1-sentence
   follow-up or question].
2. [Specific thing about team/product you're genuinely curious about] —
   would love to dig in more if there's a next round.

Attaching a quick [code sample / diagram / doc] in case useful — this
is the [project/approach] I mentioned.

— [you]
```

The attachment is the killer move. It turns you from "candidate" into "already contributing."

## 7. Interviews — Beating the Cold-Apply Grinders

You can't out-grind 800-LeetCode candidates by May. What you can do:

1. **Pick a niche, have a real project.** One deep project beats five shallow ones.
2. **Mocks, not more LeetCode.** Pramp, interviewing.io, peers.
3. **System design even as new grad.** DDIA ch. 1–3, Jordan Has No Life videos, sketch URL shortener + rate limiter.
4. **Behaviorals with STAR + metrics.** Every story ends in a number.
5. **Ask insane questions at the end.** "What's the hardest unsolved problem on the team?" / "What would 30 days look like that makes you say 'great hire'?"

## 10. Making a Startup — If You Seriously Mean It

Start one *while* job-hunting, not instead of.

**Month 1 (now → May):**
- Pick a problem you personally have this week. Not "AI for X."
- 20 user conversations, 30 min each.
- Build crappiest version in a weekend. Ship to 5.

**Month 2 (June):**
- 10 active users. Charge something ($5–$100/mo). Paying users tell the truth.
- Apply YC Summer/Fall, Neo, Z Fellows, Pear, On Deck, Antler.

**Month 3+ (July on):**
- 20 paying users or a signed LOI → raise pre-seed from angels on SAFEs ($100–500k, 5–15%).
- If not → you now have a massive differentiator for FT jobs.

**Reading (under 10 hours):** YC "How to Start a Startup" lectures 1–3, Paul Graham's "Do Things That Don't Scale," "Default Alive or Default Dead."

## 13. The Core Insight Nobody Internalizes

The job market is an **information market, not a skill market.** The best candidate doesn't get the job — the best-matched candidate the hiring manager hears about at the right moment does.

Your edge comes from four asymmetries:
1. **Information** — know about jobs before the flood
2. **Attention** — be a known name, not a resume in a pile
3. **Proof** — show work they can verify in 30 seconds
4. **Surface area** — be in more rooms where decisions happen

Every tactic below attacks one of those.

---

## 14. Information Asymmetry

Ghost jobs are 40%+ of LinkedIn postings. You want fresh, real openings.

**Signals a job is real and fresh:**
- Posted on company ATS within 48 hours
- Hiring manager posted about it personally
- Referenced in a funding announcement
- Appears in a talent-collective email (a16z, Sequoia, etc.)

**Pre-indexed discovery stack:**
1. **Funding announcements → jobs.** Google Alerts for `"raised" "Series A" "engineers"`. Reach out before jobs post.
2. **GitHub contributor graph mining.** Companies with commit spikes but small headcount are under-hired.
3. **Wellfound "active within 24h"** filter.
4. **LinkedIn Sales Navigator 30-day trial** — filter headcount growth >20% + posted in last week.
5. **Paragon One / RippleMatch / Canary** — curated student platforms.
6. **Talent collectives** — a16z TxO, Sequoia Arc, GV network, On Deck.
7. **HN "Who wants to be hired?"** — post yourself first of the month.

**Automation idea:** scraper that pulls funding announcements, filters to 10–100 person companies in your domain, generates outreach referencing the raise.

## 15. Attention Asymmetry

Six moves, hardest to easiest:

**A. Build-in-public.** One technical thing/day for 60 days. Cross-post Twitter + LinkedIn. DMs, speaker offers, searchable depth.

**B. One technical blog post per week.** Deep dives, not listicles. Own domain, not Medium. One good post ≈ one interview.

**C. Speak at a small meetup.** Organizers are desperate. Email: "I'd like to give a 15-min talk on [thing]." Instant acceptance.

**D. "Rewrite their docs" move.** Fix something in a target startup's docs/API/onboarding. Send to CTO directly. ~30% interview conversion.

**E. Product teardown email.** 1-page: 3 wins, 3 specific fixes with mockups, 1 strategic insight. Send to founder. 10–20% reply.

**F. Reply-guy strategy.** 20 founders, substantive replies for 4–6 weeks, then DM. Warm DM conversion ~40% vs <2% cold.

## 16. Proof Asymmetry

The resume is the weakest part. Minimize reliance on it.

**Better proof than a resume:**
1. Live demo URL, no signup, works in 30 seconds
2. GitHub repo with screenshots, architecture diagram, benchmarks
3. Merged PR to a well-known project
4. Real numbers with real artifacts (flamegraphs, commits, traces)
5. "Work samples" page: problem, constraints, tried, worked, would do differently
6. Recorded Loom walkthroughs

**Weekend setup:**
- Buy your name domain ($12)
- Next.js + Vercel (2 hours)
- Bio paragraph, 3 projects with demos, Calendly link, call-to-action

Top 5% on presentation alone.

## 19. Resume-Tailor — Unfair Advantage Features

### Unfair tier — build first
1. **Truthfulness diff + hallucination guard.** Red-flag any claim not in source. Defend every word.
2. **JD keyword gap visualizer.** Top 30 → missing → suggested real bullets.
3. **ATS round-trip test.** Parse generated PDF back out, show recruiter-side view.
4. **Company dossier generator.** One button → summary, news, tech stack, 3 hiring managers, 3 smart questions.
5. **Outreach generator with memory.** Uses your voice, avoids repeat phrases.
6. **Thank-you generator from interview notes.**
7. **Application tracker with smart follow-up.**
8. **"Why me for this role" drafter.** 3 alignment bullets + 1 honest gap.

### Compounding tier
9. Bullet library with usage stats — fine-tuned over time
10. Outcome tracking → A/B your resume versions
11. Saved company profiles — research once
12. Rejection pattern analysis after 50 apps

### Moat tier (if you product-ize)
13. Network graph — 2nd-degree intros you didn't know existed
14. Chrome extension re-ranking LinkedIn jobs by real fit
15. Coaching mode — builds a model of your blockers

**Build this week:** truthfulness guard + JD gap + company dossier. ~4 hours, 2–3× conversion lift.

## 21. Psychological Game

- Job hunt is a grief cycle, not a sprint. Plan for 2 bad months. Workout + 8h sleep + weekly social = non-negotiable.
- Rejection is information, not judgment. No response from 200 = funnel broken, not you.
- Don't hunt alone. 2–3 peers, weekly sync, trade leads and mocks.
- Cap doomscrolling hard. Blind / r/cscareerquestions / LinkedIn feed: 15 min/day max.
- You're not competing with "everyone who applied." You're competing with 3–5 people on the hiring manager's desk. Do the work that gets you to the desk.

## 22. The One Bet That Matters Most

If you do nothing else from this doc:

> Pick ONE company you genuinely want to work at. Spend 20 hours over 2 weeks becoming the most informed outside candidate. Build one thing that's genuinely useful to them. Send it to the CTO or a specific engineer with no ask attached. Follow up once.

That single move breaks every assumption of the hiring funnel. You stop being a candidate and become a future teammate.

Do it three times this summer. One will convert.
